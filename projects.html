LexiMind

LexiMind — multi-task NLP research & book discovery

Nov 2025 – present

LexiMind began as my undergraduate research project at Appalachian State. It now brings together an open-source transformer implementation and an independent book-discovery application.

I implemented attention, encoder and decoder layers, relative-position bias, cached generation, and task heads in PyTorch. Pretrained FLAN-T5 weights are transferred into those modules; a shared encoder supports summarization, topic classification, and emotion classification.

The current study asks whether model merging is worth it once you pay for training the experts. Under one matched training budget, it compares a jointly trained LoRA adapter, separate task specialists, and specialists merged with task arithmetic and TIES on book genre, topic, and narrative-emotion classification. Evaluation uses duplicate-aware splits of the Blurb Genre Collection, where 849 groups of matching records crossed the official splits, and reports five seeds against tuned TF-IDF baselines. The study is in progress; earlier undergraduate results remain historical and have not been reproduced.

The live book app uses content-based recommendations over sourced descriptions, subjects, and genres. It supports browser-local favourites and reading lists, and does not run the research model.

PlayAxis

Sports, training & community

PlayAxis brings event discovery, team follows, training logs, and community into one place. Browse local sport and gaming events on a map, RSVP to sessions, follow fixtures and results, or keep a private activity journal with goals and progress charts. Clubs, group sessions, and posts connect people around what they play.

A React frontend and FastAPI backend bring together sports feeds, event listings, maps, and live broadcasts. SQLAlchemy manages saved activity and community data, while provider-specific integrations keep differing schedules and data formats usable in one interface.

SartheAtlas

Le Mans, explored in 3D

An interactive explorer of Le Mans cars and the Circuit de la Sarthe. Exterior references sit alongside explorable engineering studies, with exploded views, removable bodywork, and component details. The circuit atlas brings together aerial imagery, terrain, selectable corners, and an elevation profile.

Built with React, TypeScript, Three.js, and MapLibre. Car architecture notes and sourced geography are distinguished from illustrative geometry and lap animation: the engineering studies explain how the cars work, rather than presenting themselves as exact replicas or manufacturer CAD.

Pulse

Local coding-agent usage

A local-first macOS app, menu-bar view, and browser dashboard for coding-agent usage. Pulse brings subscription allowances and reset times together with token history, model breakdowns, and estimated API-equivalent costs for tools including Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, Command Code, and OpenCode.

Built with Electron and Node.js, it reads available local transcripts and CLI metadata through a server bound to the local machine. Provider coverage depends on the installed tools and available data. Missing allowances stay unavailable, and API-equivalent estimates are kept separate from actual subscription charges.
